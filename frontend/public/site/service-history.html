Operating history

How the model evolved.

HOSUN product path

Structured lifting-system promotion, local sample context, customer-safe product resources, and quote preparation created the first repeatable workflow.

Quote and PDF discipline

Interval pricing, professional PDF output, quote numbering, draft history, and clear internal cost boundaries became required operating rules.

Order and shipment visibility

Customer-facing order dashboards, production milestones, shipment summaries, and feedback intake separated the customer view from internal execution.

JOBO education furniture

Education furniture introduced a different buying pattern: school procurement timing, classroom deployment, resource needs, project acceptance, and delivery consistency.

Supplier-neutral IntelliOpus

The current direction is an independent cross-border service bridge where each supplier keeps its own product model and the customer receives one coherent workspace.

What we learned

The service is built around repeatable decisions.

01

Customers need clarity

Product programs, samples, references, order status, and feedback must be easier to follow than the supplier-side process behind them.

02

Suppliers need separation

HOSUN lifting systems and JOBO education furniture have different product rules, but they can share one customer-facing service structure.

03

Quotes need discipline

Every quote should preserve interval pricing, customer-safe output, PDF delivery, and internal commercial assumptions.

04

Operations need memory

Feedback, delivery results, and quote outcomes should improve future product, supplier, and customer decisions.
